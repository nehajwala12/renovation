Section 01 · Overview

Customers judge the delivery line more than the price. Since 1 October, that line has become about $1.8m a year more expensive to carry.

This refresh keeps the first packet's finding and adds what has happened outside the business since: record diesel, mid-year LTL rate increases, a cabinet duty that doubles on 1 January, and mortgage rates back above 7%. It ends with a fixed-fee, ten-day sprint on the four decisions that cannot wait.

Since our 1 October preview, three outside costs have moved against the delivery line, and the sponsor's own position has changed

The first packet was dated 1 October and anchored on two facts from our call: a 7% price rise that cost no volume, and a 50% freight offer that nearly doubled revenue on freight orders. Both still stand. What has changed is the cost of the thing the customer was most sensitive to.

Caution

The asymmetry we found is now more valuable and more exposed: customers watch the freight line, and the freight line just got more expensive

The first packet said to price the product and sell the delivery. The refresh adds a second point: the delivery charge has to move with fuel, or it quietly turns into a discount.

The pre-buy decision on cabinets has to be made by mid-November, before the hearth peak is over

Duty applies on the date goods are entered. With ocean transit and entry, a purchase order that is not on the water by about 13 November will most likely land at 50%.

A fixed-fee, ten-day sprint answers the two time-boxed decisions for $20,000, and is credited in full if you extend

The first packet proposed a three-week diagnostic across all eleven storefronts. This refresh proposes starting smaller and sooner, on the decisions that cannot wait. Section 05 has the full plan.

Three decisions would start the work on Monday 19 October

Each decision is small, reversible and inside management's authority. None requires a board vote.

Section 03 · Market since 1 October

Remodeling spend is still growing, but slowly, and Harvard expects it to fade to 0.5% by mid-2027

The two outlooks the first packet used have both been updated. Neither points to a volume tailwind, which makes share gain and margin the only routes to growth.

Consumers are gloomier and expect more inflation, but some are buying durables early to beat price rises

That combination favors a retailer that can show the full delivered price early and credibly. It punishes a surprise at checkout.

Note

Michigan's survey director attributed the small improvement in durable buying conditions to consumers buying before prices rise further. For RTA Cabinet Store, a public 'prices change 1 January' message is both true and useful.

Caution

Sentiment is at a four-month low and expectations fell about 10% in September. The sprint does not assume demand holds; it sizes freight and duty at today's volumes only.

The big-box leader grew on ticket, not traffic; the online leader grew on orders, especially in specialty

Home Depot's transactions fell while its average ticket rose. Wayfair's specialty brands grew close to 20%. Both point to share moving online and to specialists, which is Renovation Brands' model.

Three of the eleven storefronts carry most of the exposure to what changed

RTA Cabinet Store is exposed to all four forces. Electric Fireplaces Direct is exposed to freight and to price comparison. The specialty brands ship mostly by parcel and are least affected.

On the model, Renovation Brands' outbound freight bill rises about 15.6%, from $11.4m to $13.2m a year

The bridge repriced the same shipments at September diesel and 2026 rates. Two-thirds of the increase is fuel surcharge. The figure is consistent with Cass's 18.7% rise in national freight spend.

The duty on imported cabinets and vanities doubles to 50% on 1 January 2027, with no exemption for Mexico or Canada

The 25% duty has applied since 14 October 2025. Proclamation 11000 delayed the step to 50% by a year; it now takes effect in 81 days. The UK, EU, Japan, South Korea and Taiwan have lower capped rates.

Each week of cabinet stock entered before 1 January avoids about $62,000 of duty, and costs about $310,000 of cash

The constraint is not carrying cost; at 15% a year it is small against a 25-point duty saving. The constraints are cash, warehouse space and the demand outlook in Section 03.
